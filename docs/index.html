When Should Multiplicative Symmetry Be Hard-Coded?

Exchange-symmetric, antisymmetric, same-token quadratic, and learnable-angle bilinear interactions in vision backbones.

Code & data: nooobkevin/exchange-symmetry-vision Precursor study: Revisiting Wedge Products in Subspace Clifford Networks

Multiplicative vision layers multiply two projected feature streams elementwise, as in StarNet, polynomial MetaFormers, and Clifford networks. Whenever a layer multiplies features from an ordered pair of tokens (u, v) using shared projections A, B, the swapped products p = (Au) ⊙ (Bv) and q = (Av) ⊙ (Bu) span two orthogonal subspaces under input exchange: a symmetric part s = (p + q) / √2 that stays unchanged when u and v swap, and an antisymmetric part a = (p − q) / √2 that flips sign.

fr(u, v) = cos(θr) pr + sin(θr) qr = cos(θr − π/4) sr + cos(θr + π/4) ar

Fixing θ = −π/4 gives pure antisymmetry (anti, the projected counterpart of a Clifford wedge product). Fixing θ = +π/4 gives pure symmetry (sym), and fixing θ = 0 gives an unconstrained Hadamard product (star). Making θ ∈ ℜR learnable (free_anti, free_star, free_sym) starts from the exact same function and step-0 gradients as the corresponding fixed model while letting gradient descent rotate each channel's symmetry continuously.

Across synthetic bilinear teachers, frozen ConvNeXt-Atto probes, paired ImageNet-1k fine-tuning, 4-way orientation prediction, and 100-epoch from-scratch ConvNeXt-Atto and ViT-Tiny runs on an 8× H800 node, we test three concrete questions: when hard-coding antisymmetry helps or hurts, what symmetry gradient descent chooses on its own, and when apparent symmetric neighbor interactions reduce to single-token quadratic features (self_avg).

1. Three algebraic laws before touching a backbone

Any bilinear form y = uT M v can be represented by unconstrained Hadamard products p = (Au) ⊙ (Bv) once the rank R is wide enough, because a wedge term ui vj − uj vi is a difference of two products. Hard-coding symmetry therefore changes sample complexity, coordinate dependence, and effective rank rather than universal expressivity.

Testing ground-truth teachers y = uT M v + ε (d = 16) across antisymmetric, symmetric, and mixed matrices exposes three clean properties, plus an instrumentation trap: individual channels in a learnable layer can have θr ≠ +π/4 while canceling their antisymmetric parts across channels in the readout Wout. Measuring post-readout functional antisymmetry via swapped inputs, FuncAnti(δ) = ||δanti||2 / (||δsym||2 + ||δanti||2), recovers the true operator symmetry to three decimal places regardless of initialization.

2. Task symmetry vs. pair geometry: a double dissociation

To test exchange symmetry inside a standard non-Clifford vision model without activation confounds, we insert a pure bilinear residual adapter (no internal SiLU or GELU) into ConvNeXt-Atto with shared RMSNorm2d. Because the adapter is strictly bilinear up to Wout, swapping u and v flips the exact residual update δ(v, u) = −δ(u, v) for anti and preserves it for sym.

Comparing horizontally directed neighbor pairs (xi,j, xi,j+1) across three paired experimental settings reveals why antisymmetry appeared useless in one regime and indispensable in another:

  • Directed neighbor + reflection-invariant ImageNet-1k classification: horizontal image flips swap left-right order while preserving the class label. During 30-epoch fine-tuning, gradient descent suppresses anti residual amplitude by 10× (RMS ratio = 0.037 vs. 0.374 for sym, even when step-0 RMS is explicitly equalized), and free_star converges across all four seeds to 99.7% functional symmetry (FuncAnti = 0.003).
  • Directed neighbor + 4-way image rotation prediction ({0°, 90°, 180°, 270°}): on the exact same backbone and images, spatial orientation now determines the label. Every indicator reverses: anti grows to RMS = 0.624 (1.5× larger than sym), zeroing the antisymmetric component at inference drops anti by −7.37% and free_star by −3.51% (15× larger than zeroing sym), and free_sym actively escapes pure symmetry (FuncAnti: 0.000 → 0.498).
  • Isotropic center-surround pair (u, DWConv5x5(u)) on ImageNet-1k: swapping center and isotropic surround is no longer a spatial reflection. Immediately, anti stops collapsing (RMS = 0.417, +0.20% over baseline, −0.91% when zeroed), matching sym and free_star.

3. Does symmetric neighbor multiplication even multiply across tokens?

Because adjacent Stage-4 tokens are correlated (cos(u, v) = 0.483), a natural skepticism arises: when sym and free_star beat the baseline during ImageNet-1k fine-tuning, did the model learn a genuine relation between two positions, or did it just use v ≈ u to compute a single-point quadratic nonlinearity?

We test this with an exact exchange-symmetric control, self_avg, which sees the exact same two tokens and computes two elementwise products without multiplying across positions:

SelfAvg(u, v) = [ (Au) ⊙ (Bu) + (Av) ⊙ (Bv) ] / √2,     SelfAvg(u, v) − Sym(u, v) = [ A(u − v) ⊙ B(u − v) ] / √2

Swapping sym to self_avg at inference on our four trained Wave A checkpoints without retraining drops 50k ImageNet-1k Top-1 by only −0.066% (compared to −0.354% when bypassing the adapter) and preserves 96.0% top-1 prediction agreement. Retraining self_avg from the same initialization across all four paired seeds matches sym and free_star on clean Top-1 (71.45% vs. 71.42% and 71.47%; paired SelfAvg − Sym = +0.04 ± 0.08%), though self_avg loses −1.35% on 0.12× low-contrast images (29.25% vs. 30.60%) where the cross-difference term A(u − v) ⊙ B(u − v) carries local contrast. During short fine-tuning, clean accuracy gains come from adding degree-2 channel gating rather than cross-token products.

4. 100-epoch from-scratch training: co-adaptation and the depth-symmetry law

Training the entire backbone and adapters jointly from scratch for 100 epochs on ImageNet-1k (24 multi-stage ConvNeXt-Atto runs + 18 ViT-Tiny runs) changes the picture in three ways that fine-tuning alone cannot see:

  • Co-adaptation unlocks anti via spatial derivative & GELU demodulation: from scratch, anti is no longer suppressed (Stage-4 RMS = 0.744, BypassΔ = −5.13%, beating none on all 3/3 seeds). Inspecting the downstream 7×7 depthwise conv block (stages[3].blocks[1]) shows how: its horizontally antisymmetric filter energy jumps 9.7× over baseline (6.86% vs. 0.71%, turning odd features into even features via odd * odd = even), while quadratic GELU rectification supplies 26.4% of post-GAP shift energy (vs. 2.7% in fine-tuning, where the pretrained 99.5%-even filter trapped anti near zero).
  • Depth hierarchy of symmetry: inside the same multi-stage network, mid-level Stage 3 (14×14) preserves substantial antisymmetry in free_star (FuncAnti = 0.375) and star (0.280), whereas late semantic Stage 4 (7×7) right before global pooling collapses toward exchange symmetry (FuncAnti = 0.049 in free_star, 0.132 in star).
  • Backbone mixer determines whether cross-token products beat self_avg: in from-scratch ConvNeXt-Atto (whose only spatial mixer is depthwise conv), every cross-token bilinear mode outperforms self_avg (star reaches 71.49 ± 0.27%, +0.58% over none and +0.30% over self_avg, with +3.61% higher low-contrast accuracy at half the products of sym/anti). In ViT-Tiny, where self-attention already performs pairwise cross-token mixing, pointwise quadratic self_avg is competitive or stronger.

5. Practical decision guide

  • Default to unconstrained star (Au) ⊙ (Bv) or self_avg unless the task is genuinely chiral/directional: star uses half the elementwise multiplications of sym/anti, needs no coordinate-group alignment, and lets its projection matrices (A, B) align automatically in late semantic stages (FuncAnti → 0.13) while preserving directional cross-terms in mid-level stages (0.28), achieving the best 100-epoch from-scratch ConvNeXt accuracy (71.49%) and low-contrast robustness.
  • Use free_star + swapped-pair FuncAnti as a zero-overhead symmetry probe: adding only R scalar angles (<0.01% parameters) doubles rank-bottlenecked capacity from R to 2R and reveals whether a layer/task wants symmetry (FuncAnti = 0.003 on ImageNet Stage-4 fine-tuning) or antisymmetry (0.625 on 4-way rotation; 0.375 in Stage 3).
  • Always include self_avg when claiming cross-token multiplicative gains: on pretrained backbones or Transformers with self-attention, apparent gains from symmetric token-pair multiplication can come entirely from single-token degree-2 polynomial capacity.
  • Hard-code anti only when inputs have physical exchange antisymmetry AND aligned coordinates (or learned projections): fixed channel-grouped wedges collapse under random orthogonal basis rotations (R² = −0.02), whereas projected anti is O(d)-invariant and excels when orientation flips the target label.
